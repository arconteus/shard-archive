# Entity

## Definition

An Entity represents identity: a referent or concept the archive needs to distinguish. Examples include a person, location, faction, item, concept, project, or unknown referent.

Entity does not contain knowledge about itself and does not imply objective existence. “The Observer” may have a stable Entity identity even when sources disagree about whether it is a creature, spacecraft, phenomenon, several unrelated things, or fiction within the world.

## Identity and Mentions

Two people with the same name remain separate Entities. Aliases may refer to one Entity, but ambiguous references must not force premature resolution.

Fragments and Claims may mention multiple Entities. Mentions support navigation, highlighting, discovery, and retrieval; they do not imply relationships. Assertions about an Entity belong in Claims.

## AI and Invariants

AI may suggest candidates, aliases, types, or merges. Creation and merging require explicit user action.

1. Entity is one of four canonical primitives.
2. Entity represents identity, not facts or ontological truth.
3. Entity mentions do not imply graph edges.
4. Ambiguity is preserved until intentionally resolved.
